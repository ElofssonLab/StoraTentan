The oral exam in Bioinformatics, Stora Tentan, 15 hp –  
some hints

Stora Tentan's purpose is that students awarded a PhD from our Department should have a good basic knowledge of Bioinformatics, knowledge wider than their own thesis area. Stora Tentan is based on the assumption that the PhD student has the general knowledge required to pass all the advanced-level courses in Bioinformatics and comparative genomics at the department, but primarily, a test of the ability to demonstrate how bioinformatics methods can be applied to solve fundamental biological questions. Furthermore, it is assumed that the student has taken part in seminars and dissertations given at DBB and SciLifeLab, getting information about the research at DBB/SciLifeLab as well as the research topics at the international level.

There are two examiners, and the exam lasts about two hours. No specific textbook is required, but Zvelebil and Baum, or Pevzner cover most of the areas. However, these books are not up to date, so the student needs to update their knowledge to the current state of the art. Here, reading the proceedings from the last few ISMB and ECCB meetings is a good start. Experience has shown that discussing the exam with other PhD students has been a very efficient way to prepare for it and that online lectures often provide better explanations than some textbooks.

Please note that Stora Tentan is not an exam where the student is expected to know all the details. Still, it should show an ability to discuss and think about bioinformatics methods, their advantages/disadvantages, their use, and their algorithms.  Fundamental knowledge of the algorithms behind and the limitations of well-established methods for sequence searching, protein structure predictions, phylogeny, and function annotations of genes and proteins is required.

Some topics that often will be discussed include:

- **Sequence searching**. Both historical and state-of-the-art methods are used for speed and accuracy. Understanding the advantages of different algorithms is essential.  
- **Databases:** Knowledge of bioinformatics most important databases and understanding their hierarchy and relationships.  
- **Evolution**. How does evolution affect the current genomes? Gene duplication, evolutionary pressure.  
- **Phylogeny**. Advantages and limitations of different methods.   
- **Genomics**. Methods for sequencing, algorithms for assembly, gene predictions, etc.   
- **Genomes**. Fundamental differences in how different genomes are constructed. What is the difference between eukaryotes and prokaryotic genomes?  
- **Expression analysis of genes and proteins**. Methods, algorithms statisitcs  
- **Proteomics**: Different proteomics methods. How are these used, algorithms  
- **Protein Structure**: Knowledge of protein structure including domains and different methods to predict protein structure  
- **Protein function**. Classifications, prediction methods, and definitions.  
- **Protein-protein interactions**: Experimental and computational methods to analyse which proteins interact and how.  
- **Machine learning**: Fundamental knowledge about machine learning. How machine learning is used in bioinformatics. Recent progress in machine learning methodologies.

Finally, it should be emphasised that every exam is unique, largely depending on the student. Depending on what is discussed in the first topic listed here, the other topics may be covered in more or less detail, even not at all.
